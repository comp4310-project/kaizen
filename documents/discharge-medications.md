# Purpose

Patients leave hospital with the medications they need, a correct list of what to take at home, and a pharmacy that already has their prescription.

# Before the day of discharge

The most responsible physician writes the discharge prescription by 16:00 on the day before the expected date of discharge.

The ward pharmacist compares the discharge prescription with the home medication list taken on admission and resolves every difference with the prescriber before the patient leaves.

The pharmacist sends the prescription to the patient's community pharmacy by the evening before discharge, so medications can be ready when the patient arrives.

# On the day of discharge

The nurse reviews each new, changed or stopped medication with the patient and family using teach-back, asking the patient to explain in their own words what each one is for and when to take it.

Patients on high-alert medications, such as anticoagulants, insulin or opioids, receive a written medication schedule before they leave the unit.

# Measures

The unit counts the share of discharges where the prescription reached the community pharmacy before the patient left, and the share of discharges delayed while waiting for medications. Both are reviewed monthly at the unit quality meeting.
