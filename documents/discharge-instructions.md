# Purpose

Patients and families understand what to do at home, what warning signs to watch for, and who to call.

# Written instructions

Every patient receives written discharge instructions in plain language that list their diagnosis, medications, activity limits, warning signs and follow-up appointments.

Instructions are given in the patient's preferred language. When a professional interpreter is needed, the nurse books one before the discharge conversation rather than relying on family members.

# Teach-back

The nurse uses teach-back for the three most important instructions and repeats the explanation until the patient or family can describe them correctly.

# Follow-up

Follow-up appointments are booked before the patient leaves and written on the instructions with the date, time and place.

A nurse phones patients discharged on new high-alert medications or with a new diagnosis within 48 hours of discharge to check their understanding and answer questions.

# Measures

The unit audits ten discharge records each month for complete written instructions, and reports the share of follow-up calls completed within 48 hours.
